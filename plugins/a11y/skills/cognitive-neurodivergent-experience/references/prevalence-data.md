# Prevalence Data: Cognitive Disabilities and Neurodivergence

## Purpose

This reference exists to counter "edge case" dismissals. When someone argues that cognitive or neurodivergent accessibility is niche, these numbers demonstrate otherwise. Every figure is cited to a specific source.

## Population Estimates

| Condition | Prevalence | Population Context | Source |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|--------|
| **Neurodivergent (any)** | 15-20% of population | 1 in 5 people | [WCAG.com](https://www.wcag.com/blog/digital-accessibility-and-neurodiversity/) |
| **Anxiety disorders** | ~19% of US adults | Nearly 1 in 5 adults | NIMH |

## Key Points for Agents

### These Are Not Edge Cases

- **15-20% of the population is neurodivergent.** That is roughly the same proportion as left-handed people. No one would design a product that only works for right-handed users.
- **19% of US adults have an anxiety disorder.** Anxiety-inducing interface patterns (countdown timers, scarcity messaging, irreversible actions without clear consequences) affect nearly one in five adult users.
- **12% of the US population has a cognitive disability.** This includes age-related cognitive decline, traumatic brain injuries, learning disabilities, and intellectual disabilities.

### Conditions Overlap

A single user may have ADHD + anxiety + dyslexia. Autism frequently co-occurs with anxiety and sensory processing differences. Age-related cognitive decline compounds with existing neurodivergent conditions. The affected population is not the sum of individual conditions — but it is larger than any single condition suggests.

### Temporary and Situational Cognitive Disability

Beyond permanent conditions, cognitive function is affected by:
- **Fatigue** — anyone after a long day
- **Stress** — anyone under deadline pressure
- **Medication** — side effects affecting concentration, memory, reaction time
- **Illness** — fever, pain, brain fog
- **Multitasking** — divided attention reduces available cognition
- **Aging** — progressive cognitive changes affect everyone

Microsoft's Inclusive Design framework models this as a spectrum: permanent (cognitive disability) -> temporary (concussion) -> situational (sleep deprivation). Solutions for permanent conditions benefit everyone on the spectrum. ([Microsoft Inclusive Design](https://inclusive.microsoft.design/))

### The Business Argument

When accessibility is framed as "helping disabled users," it can be deprioritized. When framed as "15-20% of your users cannot complete this flow because of how their brain works," it becomes a conversion and retention issue. Every inaccessible form, every anxiety-inducing timeout, every sensory-overload animation is a lost user.

## W3C COGA Personas

The W3C Cognitive and Learning Disabilities Accessibility Task Force defines personas representing the breadth of cognitive conditions:

| Persona | Condition | Key Barrier |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| Alison | Mild cognitive impairment | Needs more time; loses data to timeouts |
| George | Down syndrome | Complex language and forms |
| Maria | Memory loss | Cannot recall previously entered data |
| Kwame | Traumatic brain injury | Cognitive fatigue; needs save-and-resume |
| Stefan | Dyslexia and ADHD | Reading difficulty; focus disruption |
| Ian | Autism | Unpredictable layouts; sensory overload |
| Sophie | Down syndrome | Acronyms and abbreviations |

([W3C COGA Usable](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/), [W3C WAI User Stories](https://www.w3.org/WAI/people-use-web/user-stories/))
