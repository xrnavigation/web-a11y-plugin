# Interpreting population and user evidence

The previous population table mixed diagnoses, age groups, geographies, dates, and unsupported estimates. Do not use it to infer that a fixed percentage of this product's users cannot finish a task. WAI user stories describe examples of access needs, not population prevalence. ([WAI user stories](https://www.w3.org/WAI/people-use-web/user-stories/))

## Use evidence suited to the decision

- For a design barrier, identify the specific task demand and observed failure: “The session expired before the participant could gather documents.”
- For population estimates, retrieve a primary epidemiological source and preserve its year, geography, age group, diagnostic definition, denominator, and uncertainty. Do not add overlapping conditions together.
- For product impact, measure the actual flow. Population prevalence does not establish a conversion loss or abandonment rate.
- For research participants, distinguish their own account from an observer's interpretation. Do not infer a diagnosis from interface behavior.

These are project evidence rules. W3C's [COGA guidance](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/) supports attention to diverse cognitive needs without making diagnoses interchangeable.

## Wrong/right reasoning

Wrong: “One in five users cannot complete this checkout because they are neurodivergent.”

Right: “This checkout requires remembering earlier choices and finishing without a break. Add review/save options, and evaluate completion with participants who have relevant access needs.”

Wrong: “All dyslexic people need off-white backgrounds and sans-serif fonts.”

Right: “Test readability with the target audience, preserve contrast, and support adaptable presentation. Individual preferences differ.” ([COGA usable](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/))
